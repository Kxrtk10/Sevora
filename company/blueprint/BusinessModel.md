# Business Model

## Strategic Thesis

Sevora is a premium AI Product Studio. We create value through three connected activities:

1. building and owning intelligent software products;
2. designing custom AI-powered software for selected businesses; and
3. advising leaders on where AI can produce measurable advantage.

This is one business model, not three unrelated businesses. Advisory reveals important problems. Custom delivery proves which solutions work. Reusable knowledge and components improve execution. Owned products turn validated patterns into compounding assets.

Sevora is not an outsourcing agency. We do not sell interchangeable development capacity, accept arbitrary specifications, or compete on hourly rates. We take responsibility for understanding the problem, shaping the product, and delivering the outcome.

## The Three Engines

### 1. Owned Products

Sevora researches, validates, builds, and operates focused software products. Product investment is earned through evidence: a clear user, an important recurring problem, credible distribution, and willingness to pay.

Owned products create recurring revenue, proprietary knowledge, and long-term enterprise value. They are the destination of the model, but they must not be funded by optimism alone. The [Proof Engine](ProofEngine.md) and client work provide the evidence required to choose where to invest.

### 2. Solution Partnerships

Sevora works with a small number of businesses to solve consequential operational, customer, and decision problems. Engagements may include discovery, product design, engineering, deployment, and measured improvement.

We sell defined outcomes and accountable delivery, not staff augmentation. Each engagement must meet the qualification standards in the [Ideal Client Profile](IdealClientProfile.md) and address a problem category in [Problems We Solve](ProblemsWeSolve.md).

### 3. AI Strategy and Advisory

Advisory helps leaders identify valuable AI opportunities, understand risk, establish priorities, and create an executable roadmap. It is a standalone source of value and a disciplined entry point to larger work. Advice must remain independent; we do not recommend a build merely to create implementation revenue.

The offer architecture is defined in [Services](Services.md).

## The Compounding Loop

1. Research industries and speak with operators.
2. Demonstrate understanding through focused industry solutions.
3. Diagnose a qualified client's highest-value problem.
4. Deliver a narrow, complete solution and measure the result.
5. Convert reusable learning into methods, components, or product hypotheses.
6. Publish credible proof without exposing customer trust.
7. Reinvest revenue and evidence into owned products.

Every cycle should leave Sevora with stronger relationships, better software, deeper knowledge, and greater delivery leverage.

## Strategic Boundaries

Sevora will:

- remain selective about industries, clients, and concurrent work;
- own product direction rather than execute unexamined requirements;
- use existing tools when they solve the problem better than custom software;
- retain reusable, non-client-specific methods and components where contracts permit;
- protect dedicated capacity for product research and development; and
- decline work that cannot meet our standards for impact, trust, or quality.

## Resource Allocation

During the first three years, client revenue provides stability and market access while product investment remains evidence-led. Capacity should be explicitly allocated each quarter across client delivery, owned products, proof, and company systems. Product work must have protected time; client work must never consume the entire company by default.

The transition toward recurring revenue is described in [Revenue Model](RevenueModel.md), and the sequence is governed by the [Three-Year Roadmap](ThreeYearRoadmap.md).

## Five-Year Direction

By the end of year five, Sevora should have:

- a respected, selective solution practice;
- one or more validated products with recurring revenue;
- reusable technical and research assets that materially improve delivery;
- demonstrable expertise in a small number of industries;
- a meaningful share of revenue from products and ongoing partnerships; and
- a reputation for craftsmanship, discretion, and measurable impact.

The model succeeds when each part strengthens the others without allowing services to become generic or products to become speculative.
