# Proof Engine

## Purpose

Before Sevora has a long operating history, trust must be earned through visible evidence of judgment and execution. The Proof Engine is the system that turns research and building into credible proof, then turns market response into better strategy.

It is not a content calendar. It is a company learning system.

## The Proof Ladder

Evidence should progress in strength:

1. **Point of view:** A clear explanation of an important problem.
2. **Workflow artifact:** A process map, evaluation, teardown, or decision framework.
3. **Interactive demonstration:** A focused product experience using realistic scenarios.
4. **Working system:** Software tested with representative users and constraints.
5. **Measured outcome:** A deployed result with an honest baseline and evidence.
6. **Independent trust:** A customer reference, repeat engagement, or referral.

Sevora must label each level accurately. A concept is not a customer result, and a demo is not a production product.

## Demo Industry Solutions

Industry demos make Sevora's understanding inspectable before a customer engagement.

### ClinicFlow

A healthcare operations demonstration focused on patient intake, appointment coordination, follow-up, document handling, and operational visibility. It should emphasize privacy, human review, and workflow reliability rather than clinical diagnosis.

### RestaurantOS

A restaurant operations demonstration focused on reservations or orders, inventory signals, staff coordination, customer recovery, and daily decision support. It should show how a unified workflow reduces missed information and management overhead.

Future demos should be created only after operator interviews identify a repeated, consequential problem. One excellent vertical demonstration is more valuable than many shallow dashboards.

## Demo Standard

Every industry demo must include:

- a named user and observed problem;
- a realistic end-to-end workflow;
- representative, synthetic data;
- clear AI and human responsibilities;
- failure, empty, and review states;
- a short architecture and security note;
- defined outcome measures; and
- an explicit label: concept, prototype, pilot, or production.

## Proof Production Cycle

1. Select one priority problem from [Problems We Solve](ProblemsWeSolve.md).
2. Interview at least five relevant operators or users.
3. Document the current workflow and economic cost.
4. Build the smallest demonstration that resolves the core uncertainty.
5. Test it with representative users and record what changed.
6. Publish the problem, decisions, demonstration, and limitations.
7. Use response to choose: deepen, revise, partner, productize, or stop.

## Client Proof

With permission, each suitable engagement should produce a concise case study covering context, baseline, intervention, measured result, and customer perspective. Confidentiality takes precedence over marketing. Anonymous evidence may be used only when it remains specific and verifiable.

## Proof Metrics

Review monthly:

- qualified conversations generated by each proof asset;
- operator interviews completed;
- demos tested with target users;
- progression from demo to paid discovery;
- case studies with measured outcomes;
- repeat clients and referrals; and
- product hypotheses advanced or retired.

Distribution and presentation are governed by [Website Strategy](WebsiteStrategy.md). Proof informs client qualification under [Ideal Client Profile](IdealClientProfile.md), but never replaces discovery.
