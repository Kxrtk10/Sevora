# Core Values

Values are standards for behavior, especially when the decision is difficult, costly, or unseen. They apply to how we build, lead, hire, sell, and serve.

## 1. Purpose Before Novelty

We pursue meaningful progress, not technological spectacle. A new capability matters only when it improves a real outcome.

**In practice:** We define the human or business problem before selecting tools. We remove features that add complexity without value.

## 2. Raise the Standard

We improve the quality of everything entrusted to us. Excellence is not perfectionism; it is disciplined care for what matters.

**In practice:** We review the details, challenge work that is merely adequate, and leave products, systems, and documents better than we found them.

## 3. Earn Trust

Trust is built through honest communication, sound judgment, and consistent execution. We protect it above speed, convenience, and short-term revenue.

**In practice:** We keep commitments, disclose risks early, admit mistakes directly, and never promise what we cannot responsibly deliver.

## 4. Serve Human Potential

Technology should increase human capability and preserve human dignity. Our work should help people exercise better judgment, create more, and spend time more meaningfully.

**In practice:** We design AI to assist rather than diminish agency. We ask who becomes more capable because a product exists.

## 5. Practice Craftsmanship

Quality is the result of attention repeated across thousands of decisions. We care about how something works, how it feels, and how well it endures.

**In practice:** We study the problem, design coherently, engineer for reliability, and refine the final details rather than treating them as optional.

## 6. Think in Decades

We make decisions that preserve the company's ability to create value over time. We do not trade reputation, culture, or product integrity for temporary advantage.

**In practice:** We favor durable systems, patient relationships, and sustainable economics. We consider the second-order effects of important choices.

## 7. Seek Clarity

Clear thinking precedes good execution. We make assumptions visible, write down reasoning, and reduce unnecessary complexity.

**In practice:** We define terms, identify the decision owner, explain tradeoffs, and turn ambiguity into a testable next step.

## 8. Take Ownership

We accept responsibility for outcomes, not only assigned tasks. Problems do not become someone else's merely because they cross a boundary.

**In practice:** We follow work through, raise concerns with proposed solutions, and repair what we break.

## 9. Improve Continuously

No product, process, or person is finished. We remain curious, receive evidence without defensiveness, and convert learning into better practice.

**In practice:** We seek candid feedback, study failures, revisit assumptions, and make small improvements part of normal work.

## 10. Build Others Up

Our success should increase opportunity for customers, colleagues, partners, and communities. Capability shared is capability multiplied.

**In practice:** We teach what we know, give credit generously, create room for others to grow, and build systems that reduce dependence on any one person.
