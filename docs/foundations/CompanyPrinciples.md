# Company Principles

Principles guide judgment where rules cannot. They are ordered for clarity, not priority; several will often apply to the same decision.

## Purpose and Product

1. **Technology serves people.** Human benefit is the reason to build, not a constraint added later.
2. **Solve problems, not tickets.** Understand the outcome behind a request before implementing it.
3. **Every product should make someone better.** Increase capability, clarity, confidence, or time.
4. **Start with truth.** Observe actual behavior, constraints, and evidence before forming a solution.
5. **Design before development.** Resolve the problem, experience, and tradeoffs before committing engineering effort.
6. **Use AI where it earns its place.** Intelligence must improve the result enough to justify its cost, risk, and complexity.
7. **Simple is the result of understanding.** Remove complexity from the user's experience, even when doing so requires harder work from us.

## Quality and Execution

8. **Raise the standard.** Each piece of work should improve the quality expected from the next.
9. **Craft is part of the function.** Reliability, language, speed, accessibility, and detail shape whether a product is truly useful.
10. **Build the smallest complete solution.** Reduce scope without reducing coherence, trust, or usefulness.
11. **Speed comes from clarity.** Clear ownership, decisions, and priorities create sustainable pace.
12. **Finish the work.** A task is complete when the intended outcome is delivered, verified, documented, and maintainable.
13. **Measure outcomes, not motion.** Activity is evidence of effort, not evidence of value.
14. **Make quality repeatable.** Convert lessons into systems, tools, and standards rather than relying on memory or heroics.

## Trust and Responsibility

15. **Build trust through execution.** Keep promises, communicate early, and let consistent results establish our reputation.
16. **Tell the truth early.** Bad news becomes more expensive when delayed.
17. **Privacy and security are design requirements.** Protect people and their information from the first decision onward.
18. **Respect the customer's reality.** Their constraints, expertise, and reputation deserve serious attention.
19. **Do not create dependence unnecessarily.** Leave customers and colleagues more capable than before.

## Company and Time

20. **Think long-term; act concretely.** Hold a generational ambition and make it real through today's disciplined choices.
21. **Protect focus.** Every commitment consumes attention that cannot be spent twice.
22. **Products before trends.** Build for enduring needs and adopt new technology with judgment.
23. **Relationships compound.** Prefer durable trust with customers, colleagues, and partners over transactional gain.
24. **Document decisions, not every conversation.** Preserve context future teams will need without creating bureaucracy.
25. **Disagree with respect; commit with discipline.** Seek the best decision openly, then execute it together.
26. **Leave it better.** Improve the code, the process, the relationship, or the understanding whenever the work passes through your hands.
27. **Steward the institution.** Make decisions as though Sevora must remain worthy of trust after all of us are gone.
