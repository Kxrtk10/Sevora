# Ideal Client Profile

## Purpose

Sevora serves organizations where better software can create meaningful, measurable change. The ideal client profile protects our focus, quality, and reputation by defining which relationships deserve pursuit.

This is a qualification standard, not a description of every organization that could buy software.

## Primary Profile

Our strongest early client is a founder-led or professionally managed business with:

- a proven operation and approximately 20 to 500 employees;
- recurring workflows involving customers, staff, decisions, or operational data;
- a costly problem that current software does not solve well;
- an accountable executive sponsor with authority to act;
- access to users, process knowledge, and relevant data;
- willingness to change the operating process, not only add a tool; and
- budget appropriate for strategy, design, engineering, and responsible deployment.

The company does not need an internal AI team. It must have domain knowledge, operational ownership, and the ability to participate in discovery.

## Priority Client Situations

Prioritize businesses experiencing one or more of the following:

1. Growth has made manual coordination unreliable.
2. Important decisions depend on fragmented or delayed information.
3. Customer demand is lost through slow response or inconsistent follow-up.
4. Skilled employees spend substantial time on repetitive administrative work.
5. Existing systems create duplicate work, poor visibility, or avoidable errors.
6. Leadership sees AI opportunity but needs an independent, executable strategy.

These situations map to the categories in [Problems We Solve](ProblemsWeSolve.md).

## Early Industry Focus

Sevora should begin with industries where workflows are repeated, the cost of friction is visible, and operators can validate solutions quickly. Initial exploration includes healthcare operations, restaurants and hospitality, retail, manufacturing, education, and selected enterprise functions.

Industry focus is earned through interviews, demos, and successful delivery. It should remain narrow enough for knowledge to compound. Demo solutions such as **ClinicFlow** and **RestaurantOS** make our hypotheses tangible before we claim specialization; see [Proof Engine](ProofEngine.md).

## Buyer Profile

The effective buyer is usually a founder, owner, CEO, COO, business-unit leader, or transformation leader who:

- owns the business outcome;
- values clear thinking and direct communication;
- can provide access to real users and decisions;
- accepts that discovery may change the initial request;
- understands that premium quality requires investment; and
- seeks a long-term technology partner rather than inexpensive capacity.

## Qualification Scorecard

Before proposing a substantial engagement, score each dimension from 0 to 2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem severity | Optional | Noticeable | Material and urgent |
| Outcome clarity | Vague | Directional | Measurable |
| Executive ownership | Absent | Supportive | Accountable sponsor |
| User and data access | Blocked | Partial | Available |
| Readiness to change | Tool-only | Uncertain | Operational commitment |
| Economic fit | Price-led | Budget unclear | Funds quality |
| Strategic learning | One-off | Some reuse | Strong domain value |
| Trust and values | Concern | Unknown | Strong alignment |

A score below 11 of 16 normally means decline, refer, or begin with a small paid diagnostic. Any serious concern about integrity, data use, or respect is disqualifying regardless of score.

## Poor Fits

Sevora should decline:

- staff augmentation and build-only outsourcing requests;
- buyers selecting primarily on the lowest price;
- vague demands to "add AI" without an owned problem;
- speculative demos presented as production mandates;
- projects without access to users, data, or a decision-maker;
- organizations unwilling to address privacy, security, or change management; and
- work that conflicts with the company [Non-Negotiables](../foundations/NonNegotiables.md).

## First Engagement

The default first engagement is a paid discovery or focused product sprint. It gives both parties evidence of working fit, clarifies economics, and produces an independent useful result. Progression into a build is earned, not assumed.
