# Revenue Model

## Objective

Sevora's revenue model must fund excellent work today while increasing the share of income that is recurring, defensible, and connected to owned intellectual property.

Revenue quality matters as much as revenue quantity. We prefer clear economics, aligned incentives, low concentration risk, and relationships that deepen our understanding of valuable problems.

## Revenue Streams

### 1. Strategy and Discovery

Paid diagnostic engagements, AI opportunity assessments, and product strategy sprints. These produce decision clarity, prioritized opportunities, risk analysis, and an implementation roadmap.

This revenue is high-trust and expertise-led. It also protects both parties from committing to a build before the problem is understood. Relevant offers are defined in [Services](Services.md).

### 2. Custom Product Builds

Fixed-scope or milestone-based engagements for AI-enabled workflows, internal tools, customer experiences, and decision systems. Fees cover product strategy, design, engineering, deployment, and agreed validation.

Build revenue should be substantial enough to support senior attention and production quality. Sevora does not sell isolated developer hours.

### 3. Ongoing Product Partnerships

Recurring fees for operation, monitoring, maintenance, model evaluation, security updates, analytics, and prioritized improvement. These agreements begin only after ownership boundaries and service levels are clear.

Retainers must purchase defined access, capacity, or outcomes. They are not vague availability contracts.

### 4. Owned Product Revenue

Subscriptions, usage-based charges, enterprise licenses, or carefully selected transaction fees from Sevora products. The pricing unit should follow the customer's value and the product's cost structure.

Owned product revenue is the long-term compounding engine. Product investment and monetization gates are governed by the [Three-Year Roadmap](ThreeYearRoadmap.md).

### 5. Strategic Licensing

Where appropriate, Sevora may license proven components, workflows, or industry systems to multiple operators. Licensing is used only when it preserves product quality, supportability, and intellectual-property clarity.

## Revenue Mix Direction

The mix should evolve without forcing premature product revenue:

| Period | Primary economic objective | Directional mix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1 | Establish credibility and cash discipline | Mostly discovery and builds; early recurring support |
| Year 2 | Add repeatability and recurring relationships | Balanced builds, partnerships, and first product revenue |
| Year 3 | Prove a compounding product engine | Growing product and recurring revenue; more selective builds |
| Years 4-5 | Scale what has earned conviction | Products and recurring partnerships form the majority |

These are strategic directions, not quotas. Evidence should determine the pace.

## Economic Rules

- No unpaid discovery disguised as sales.
- No engagement begins without scope, commercial terms, ownership, and acceptance criteria.
- Payment schedules should protect working capital and match delivery risk.
- A single client should not become structurally indispensable.
- Gross margin must fund senior craft, quality assurance, internal systems, and product investment.
- Recurring commitments must account for real support and infrastructure costs.
- Product pricing must include AI usage, reliability, security, and customer success economics.

## Reinvestment

After operating obligations and prudent reserves, Sevora should reinvest in:

1. owned product validation and development;
2. reusable engineering and design systems;
3. proof assets and distribution;
4. exceptional talent; and
5. security, reliability, and company infrastructure.

This allocation connects revenue to the long-term model described in [Business Model](BusinessModel.md), rather than allowing client demand to set the company's entire agenda.

## Measures

Review quarterly:

- revenue by stream and client;
- recurring revenue share;
- gross margin by engagement type;
- customer concentration;
- qualified pipeline coverage;
- collection time and cash reserve;
- product research and development investment; and
- revenue attributable to repeat clients and referrals.

Pricing decisions follow [Pricing Philosophy](PricingPhilosophy.md). Proof and pipeline creation follow [Proof Engine](ProofEngine.md) and [Website Strategy](WebsiteStrategy.md).
