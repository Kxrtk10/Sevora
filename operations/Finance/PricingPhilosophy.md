# Pricing Philosophy

## Position

Sevora prices for the responsibility of solving an important problem well.

Premium pricing is not a branding exercise. It funds senior attention, research, product design, engineering quality, security, reliable operation, and the willingness to tell a client when a proposed investment is unnecessary. Our price must be supported by a serious problem, a clear engagement, and credible execution.

## Principles

### Price the Engagement, Not the Labor

We do not sell hourly developer capacity. Strategy and build work is normally priced by defined scope, milestones, risk, and expected value. Internal effort estimates inform feasibility and margin but do not become the customer proposition.

### Discovery Has Independent Value

When the problem, workflow, data, or economics are uncertain, discovery is a paid engagement. It produces decisions and artifacts the client can use whether or not Sevora builds the solution.

### Value Sets the Ceiling; Delivery Sets the Floor

The client's plausible economic benefit defines what an investment can responsibly support. The full cost of excellent delivery, risk, and ongoing obligations defines the minimum. If no sound range exists between them, we reduce scope, change the approach, or decline.

### Reduce Scope Before Quality

Budget pressure should remove lower-value functionality, shorten the initial workflow, or stage delivery. It should not silently remove security, testing, product coherence, or essential discovery.

### Make Uncertainty Visible

Proposals state assumptions, exclusions, client responsibilities, change rules, ownership, third-party costs, and acceptance criteria. Contingency is priced where uncertainty cannot be removed.

### Align Recurring Fees With Recurring Responsibility

Ongoing fees correspond to defined operation, monitoring, support, improvement capacity, usage, or service levels. We do not use indefinite retainers to conceal unclear work.

## Commercial Structures

Preferred structures include:

- fixed-fee assessments and discovery sprints;
- milestone-based fixed fees for defined builds;
- monthly product evolution partnerships with explicit capacity and service levels;
- subscription or usage pricing for owned products; and
- selective value-linked components where outcomes are measurable and both parties control the relevant variables.

Hourly billing may be used internally or for tightly bounded exceptional work, but it is not Sevora's market position.

## Proposal Standard

Every proposal must explain:

1. the problem and why it matters;
2. the intended outcome and baseline measure;
3. the approach, scope, and deliverables;
4. responsibilities and decision owners;
5. risks, assumptions, and exclusions;
6. timeline and acceptance criteria;
7. investment and payment schedule;
8. ownership, privacy, and support terms; and
9. what happens after completion.

## Discount Policy

Sevora does not discount because a buyer asks reflexively. A lower price must correspond to a real change: reduced scope, lower urgency, reusable rights, a strategically valuable pilot, or improved payment terms.

Equity is not a substitute for payment in normal client work. Any exception requires exceptional alignment, due diligence, and explicit leadership approval.

## Pricing Governance

Review after every engagement:

- estimated versus actual delivery cost;
- scope changes and their causes;
- margin and cash timing;
- customer outcome and perceived value;
- support burden; and
- reusable learning created.

This evidence should improve future pricing without turning proposals into formulas.
